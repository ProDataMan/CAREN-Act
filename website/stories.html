In our own words

Statistics persuade some people. Stories reach everyone else. Many people who lived through segregation, the Great Migration, urban renewal, deportation raids or a police call made out of prejudice are still alive. Their memories are evidence, and when they are gone, they are gone. Share your own story, or record an elder's while you can.

Share a story

An editor reads every story before it appears. That can take a few days.

How to record an elder's story

1. Ask, and explain

  • Ask if they're willing, and tell them how it will be used: for the family, and, only if they agree, on this site.
  • Let them choose a name to show, or none.
  • They can stop, skip a question, or take something back at any time.

2. Set up

  • A phone's voice recorder is enough. Sit close, in a quiet room, away from the TV and the kitchen.
  • Start by saying the date, the place, and both your names.
  • Bring old photos or documents. They bring back memories.

3. Questions to start with

  • Where and when did you grow up? What was your street like?
  • What were the rules, written or unwritten, about where you could go?
  • Was there a time you or someone you knew was stopped, accused or turned away?
  • Did your family move? Why, and what did you leave behind?
  • What changed in your lifetime, and what didn't?
  • What do you want your grandchildren to know?

4. Afterward

  • Thank them, and give them a copy.
  • Write a short summary and share it above if they agree. Upload the audio somewhere you control and paste the link.
  • Archives such as StoryCorps and the Library of Congress also accept recordings.
