Support the Federal CAREN Act

Making discriminatory 911 calls illegal nationwide. Based on San Francisco's CAREN Act (Police Code §637).

About the Map & Data Layers

Layers are illustrative or linked to public sources. They help identify areas for prioritization where multiple factors (historical violence, residual discriminatory language, reported hate incidents) overlap. Full datasets are large; this prototype links to authoritative sources and uses sample markers. Expand with GeoJSON for production.

Prioritization note: Locations with high historical lynching counts + residual “zombie” discriminatory ordinances + documented race-motivated 911 patterns or elevated hate crime reporting merit focused advocacy attention. Overlaps do not prove current illegal enforcement but highlight places where residual effects and risk of biased emergency reporting may be higher.
  • Hate Crimes: FBI UCR data (race/ethnicity bias is the largest category). Explore full agency/state data: FBI Crime Data Explorer.
  • Lynchings: Equal Justice Initiative documented >4,000 racial terror lynchings (1877–1950), concentrated in the South. Interactive map: EJI Lynching in America.
  • Historical southern context: Documented slavery and Jim Crow systems. Most formal statutes repealed or struck down.
  • Sundown histories & residual ordinances: James Loewen’s Historical Database of Sundown Towns (thousands of places that were deliberately exclusionary for decades). See sundown.tougaloo.edu / justice.tougaloo.edu. Some municipalities still have obsolete (“zombie”) segregation-era or sundown-related language in codes until actively repealed (examples include Boynton Beach, FL, which repealed three in 2024). These are unenforceable under the Equal Protection Clause, Civil Rights Act, and Fair Housing Act; enforcement would be illegal. Redlining: Mapping Inequality (HOLC maps).
  • 911 race-motivated calls: No comprehensive national geocoded public dataset. Track via news compilations, local FOIA, or incident reports. The proposed federal law would create a clearer civil remedy.
  • Allies: Research House/Senate Judiciary members and others with civil rights sponsorship records. Use congress.gov and voting scorecards.
