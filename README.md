# CAREN Act Federal Advocacy

This repository supports efforts to introduce and pass a federal version of San Francisco's Caution Against Racial and Exploitative Non-Emergencies (CAREN) Act.

The original San Francisco ordinance (Police Code §637, Ordinance No. 219-20) makes it unlawful to knowingly cause a peace officer to contact a person with the specific intent to discriminate on the basis of protected characteristics (race, color, ancestry, ethnicity, national origin, place of birth, sex, age, religion, creed, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, weight, or height) and creates a civil cause of action for damages (minimum $1,000 + fees + possible punitive damages).

## Contents

- `docs/SF-CAREN-Act.pdf` — Original San Francisco ordinance
- `website/` — Interactive advocacy website with map layers
- Draft federal bill language (coming soon)

## Goal

Make similar protections against discriminatory emergency calls available nationwide through federal legislation so the practice is illegal in every city in every state.

## Disclaimer

Map layers use publicly available historical and statistical data for educational and advocacy purposes. They illustrate patterns from sources such as the Equal Justice Initiative (lynchings 1877–1950), FBI Uniform Crime Reporting (hate crimes), Mapping Inequality / HOLC (redlining), and historical research on sundown towns (e.g., James Loewen). Formal sundown ordinances have been repealed; claims of current operational "sundown laws" or active Jim Crow statutes on the books require specific verification, as most have been struck down or repealed. Always verify with primary sources.
